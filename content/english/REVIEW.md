**Summary (the note)**

> We use the Simple Present tense for habits and for things that are always true: “I go to school every day.” “The sun rises in the east.”
> Affirmative: I, you, we, they + verb: “We sweep the yard.” He, she, it + verb + s: “Bih sweeps the yard.” Spelling: wash → washes, go → goes, carry → carries, have → has.
> Negative: don't or doesn't + verb: “I don't drink coffee.” “Ayuk doesn't play football.” (Not: doesn't plays.)
> Interrogative (a question): Do or Does + subject + verb: “Do you like fufu?” “Does Mih sell oranges?” Short answers: “Yes, I do.” “No, she doesn't.”
> Signal words: every day, always, usually, often, sometimes, never, on Saturdays.

## Lesson 1: Speaking- Dialogue to introduce oneself and other persons

Prerequisites: none · Skill: e1 (Introducing oneself and others)

**Note** (draft)

> When we meet someone for the first time, we greet and introduce ourselves.
> • Greet: “Good morning.” “Good afternoon.” “Good evening.” With friends: “Hello” or “Hi”.
> • Introduce yourself: “My name is Bih. I am from Kumbo. I am in Form One.”
> • Introduce another person: “This is my friend Ayuk.” “Mummy, meet Tabe, my classmate.”
> • Reply: “Nice to meet you.” “Nice to meet you too.” “Pleased to meet you.”
> • Ask: “What is your name?” “Where are you from?” “How are you?”
> With an older person or a teacher, greet first, add “sir” or “madam”, and use “Good morning” rather than “Hi”.

## Lesson 2: Vocabulary- Words related to basic daily needs/respect spelling rules and conventions

Prerequisites: none · Skill: e2 (Daily-needs words; capital letters and spelling)

**Note** (draft)

> Basic daily needs are the things we need every day to live well: food, water, clothing, shelter (a home), soap, sleep and health care.
> Useful words: rice, beans, plantains, bread, oil, salt, soap, toothbrush, toothpaste, bucket, blanket, mattress, sandals, uniform.
> Spelling conventions:
> • Use a capital letter for the names of people and places, for days and months, and for the word I: Ayuk, Bamenda, Monday, October.
> • Start every sentence with a capital letter.
> • Do not use a capital letter for ordinary words in the middle of a sentence: rice, soap, market.
> • Learn words that are often misspelt: Wednesday, February, vegetables, necessary, toothpaste.
> • Clothes are what we wear; cloths are pieces of cloth.
> Check every word you write. A dictionary can help.

## Lesson 3: Grammar- The Simple Present tense (affirmative, negative and interrogative)

Prerequisites: none · Skill: e3 (The Simple Present tense)

**Note** (draft)

> We use the Simple Present tense for habits and for things that are always true: “I go to school every day.” “The sun rises in the east.”
> Affirmative: I, you, we, they + verb: “We sweep the yard.” He, she, it + verb + s: “Bih sweeps the yard.” Spelling: wash → washes, go → goes, carry → carries, have → has.
> Negative: don't or doesn't + verb: “I don't drink coffee.” “Ayuk doesn't play football.” (Not: doesn't plays.)
> Interrogative (a question): Do or Does + subject + verb: “Do you like fufu?” “Does Mih sell oranges?” Short answers: “Yes, I do.” “No, she doesn't.”
> Signal words: every day, always, usually, often, sometimes, never, on Saturdays.

## Lesson 7: Grammar- The Present continuous tense

Prerequisites: 3 · Skill: e7 (The Present continuous tense)

**Note** (draft)

> We use the Present continuous tense for actions happening now, at the moment of speaking: “Look! Bih is carrying a basin of water.”
> Form: am / is / are + verb-ing. I am reading. She is cooking. They are playing.
> Spelling of -ing: most verbs add -ing (read → reading); drop a final e (make → making); double the last letter after one short vowel (run → running, sit → sitting); ie becomes y (lie → lying).
> Negative: am not / isn't / aren't + verb-ing: “Ayuk isn't sleeping.”
> Question: Am / Is / Are + subject + verb-ing? “Are you listening?” Short answers: “Yes, I am.” “No, it isn't.”
> Signal words: now, at the moment, Look!, Listen!
> Compare: “Mih sells oranges every day.” (a habit: Simple Present) and “Mih is selling oranges now.” (now: Present continuous)

## Lesson 10: Vocabulary-countable and uncountable nouns;

**Note** (draft)

## Lesson 11: Grammar- The Simple Past tense

Prerequisites: 3, 5 · Skill: e11 (The Simple Past tense)

**Note** (draft)

> We use the Simple Past tense for actions that finished in the past: yesterday, last week, last year, two days ago.
> Regular verbs add -ed: walk → walked, cook → cooked. Spelling: like → liked, carry → carried, stop → stopped, travel → travelled.
> Irregular verbs have their own past form: go → went, eat → ate, buy → bought, see → saw, take → took. Learn them by heart.
> The past form is the same for every subject: I went, she went, they went. (Be: I, he, she, it was; we, you, they were.)
> Negative: didn't + the verb (no -ed): “Ayuk didn't go to school yesterday.” (Not: didn't went.)
> Question: Did + subject + the verb? “Did you see the match?” Short answers: “Yes, I did.” “No, I didn't.”

## Lesson 15: Grammar- The Present Perfect tense

Prerequisites: 11 · Skill: e15 (The Present Perfect tense)

**Note** (draft)

> We use the Present Perfect tense for past actions that are linked to now: “I have finished my homework.” (It is done now.) “Ayuk has lost his pen.” (He does not have it now.)
> Form: have / has + past participle. I, you, we, they have; he, she, it has.
> Regular verbs: the past participle is the same as the past: cook → cooked.
> Irregular verbs have their own form: go → gone, eat → eaten, see → seen, write → written, take → taken, buy → bought.
> Useful words: ever, never, already, just, yet. “Have you ever eaten koki?” “I have never seen the sea.” “Has Bih finished yet?”
> Negative: haven't / hasn't + participle. Question: Have / Has + subject + participle?
> With a finished time (yesterday, last week), use the Simple Past: “I saw him yesterday.”

**Card c15-1** (draft)

> The Present Perfect links a past action to now. Form: have or has + the past participle.
>
> _I have finished my homework. (It is done now.)_  
> _Ayuk has lost his pen. (He does not have it now.)_
