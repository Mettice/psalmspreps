**Summary (the note)**

> We use the Simple Present for habits (“I walk to school”), facts (“Water boils at 100 °C”) and timetables (“The bus leaves at seven”).
> With he, she, it or one person or thing, add -s or -es: he walks, she washes, it carries → carries, have → has.
> Negative: don't / doesn't + the verb without -s: “Tabe doesn't take the bus.”
> Questions: Do / Does + subject + verb: “Does the bus stop here?” — “Yes, it does.”
> Adverbs of frequency (always, usually, often, sometimes, rarely, never) go before the main verb but after be: “She always takes a taxi.” “The bus is never late.”
> Some verbs are not used in the -ing form when they mean a state: know, like, want, need, believe. Say “I know him”, not “I am knowing him”.

## Lesson 2: Vocabulary- Words related to travel and basic spelling rules

Prerequisites: 1 · Skill: e2-2 (Travel words and basic spelling rules)

**Note** (draft)

> Travel words: a journey (going from one place to another), the departure (leaving), the arrival (coming), the destination (the place you are going to), the timetable (the times of buses or trains), a passenger, the fare, a ticket, luggage, a delay (being late).
> Spelling rules when we add an ending:
> • A word ending in silent e drops the e before -ing: ride → riding, arrive → arriving (but not before -s: arrives).
> • One short vowel + one consonant, stressed at the end: double it: stop → stopping, plan → planned. British English also doubles l: travel → travelling.
> • Consonant + y changes y to i: carry → carries, carried; but vowel + y keeps the y: journey → journeys, delay → delayed.
> • A word ending in -ie changes ie to y before -ing: lie → lying.

## Lesson 3: Grammar- The Simple Present tense

Prerequisites: none · Skill: e2-3 (The Simple Present tense)

**Note** (draft)

> We use the Simple Present for habits (“I walk to school”), facts (“Water boils at 100 °C”) and timetables (“The bus leaves at seven”).
> With he, she, it or one person or thing, add -s or -es: he walks, she washes, it carries → carries, have → has.
> Negative: don't / doesn't + the verb without -s: “Tabe doesn't take the bus.”
> Questions: Do / Does + subject + verb: “Does the bus stop here?” — “Yes, it does.”
> Adverbs of frequency (always, usually, often, sometimes, rarely, never) go before the main verb but after be: “She always takes a taxi.” “The bus is never late.”
> Some verbs are not used in the -ing form when they mean a state: know, like, want, need, believe. Say “I know him”, not “I am knowing him”.

## Lesson 4: Writing- Write a paragraph on how you travelled by bus

Prerequisites: 1, 3 · Skill: e2-4 (Writing a paragraph about a bus journey)

**Note** (draft)

> A paragraph is a group of sentences about one main idea. It starts on a new line.
> • The topic sentence comes first and says what the paragraph is about: “Last December I travelled from Kumba to Douala by bus.”
> • The supporting sentences give the details in order: buying the ticket, the departure, what happened on the way, the arrival.
> • Use sequence words: first, then, after that, later, finally.

## Lesson 7: Grammar- The Present continuous tense

Prerequisites: 3 · Skill: e2-7 (The Present Continuous tense)

**Note** (draft)

> We use the Present Continuous for actions happening now (“The nurse is checking my temperature”), for things happening around now (“I am reading a book about health this week”) and for plans already fixed (“I am seeing the dentist on Friday”).
> Form: am / is / are + verb-ing. I am waiting. She is waiting. They are waiting.
> Spelling: write → writing, sit → sitting, lie → lying.
> Negative: am not / isn't / aren't + -ing: “He isn't sleeping.”
> Question: Am / Is / Are + subject + -ing: “Are you coming?” — “Yes, I am.”
> State verbs (know, like, want, need, believe, own) are not used with -ing: say “I need a doctor”, not “I am needing”.
> Signal words: now, right now, at the moment, today, this week, Look!, Listen!

**Card c7-1** (draft)

## Lesson 11: Grammar- The Simple Past tense

Prerequisites: 3 · Skill: e2-11 (The Simple Past tense)

**Note** (draft)

> We use the Simple Past for finished actions at a time in the past: yesterday, last week, in 2020, two days ago.
> Regular verbs add -ed: visit → visited, stay → stayed, stop → stopped, carry → carried, arrive → arrived.
> Irregular verbs have their own past forms: go → went, take → took, see → saw, feel → felt, buy → bought, catch → caught, drink → drank.
> The past form is the same for every person: I went, she went, they went.
> Negative: didn't + the base verb: “She didn't go to school.” (not: didn't went)
> Questions: Did + subject + base verb: “Did you take the medicine?” — “Yes, I did.”
> Be: I/he/she/it was; you/we/they were.

## Lesson 15: Grammar- The Present Perfect tense

Prerequisites: 11 · Skill: e2-15 (The Present Perfect tense)

**Note** (draft)

> We use the Present Perfect for:
> • past actions whose result matters now: “It has rained, so the ground is wet.”
> • experiences, with ever and never: “Have you ever seen snow?” — “No, I have never seen snow.”
> • actions that started in the past and go on now, with since and for: “It has rained since Monday.” “I have lived here for two years.”
> Form: have / has + past participle. I have seen, she has seen. Participles: visit → visited, see → seen, eat → eaten, write → written, go → gone.
> Negative: haven't / hasn't: “He hasn't finished yet.” Questions: “Have you finished?” — “Yes, I have.”
> Since + a point in time (since Monday, since 2020); for + a length of time (for three days).
> Do not use it with a finished time: say “I saw him yesterday”, not “I have seen him yesterday”.

**Card c15-1** (draft)

## Lesson 17: Speaking and Listening- Talk about telephones and brands of phones

Prerequisites: none · Skill: e2-17 (Talking on the telephone)

**Note** (draft)

> Phones: a mobile phone (cell phone) can call, send text messages, take photos and use the internet; a landline stays in one place. People choose a phone for its price, battery, camera and memory.
> Telephone language:
> • Answer: “Hello, Mount Fako Clinic. How can I help you?”
> • Say who you are: “This is Ebai.” (not “I am Ebai” or “Here is Ebai”)
> • Ask for someone: “May I speak to Nurse Manyi, please?”
> • Wait: “Hold on, please.” / “Please hold.”
> • Messages: “Can I take a message?” “Could you tell her …?”

> • End: “I'll call back later. Goodbye.”

## Lesson 19: Grammar- The Simple future tense

Prerequisites: 3 · Skill: e2-19 (The Simple Future tense)

**Note** (draft)

> We talk about the future in several ways:
> • will + the base verb, for predictions, promises and decisions made now: “It will rain tomorrow.” “I'll call you tonight.” (I'll = I will)
> • am / is / are going to + the base verb, for plans already made and for what we can see coming: “I'm going to buy a phone on Saturday.” “Look at the clouds: it's going to rain.”
> • Shall I / Shall we, for offers and suggestions: “Shall I carry your bag?” “Shall we go?”
> Will is the same for every person: I will, she will, they will.
> Negative: will not = won't: “He won't come.” Questions: “Will you help me?” — “Yes, I will.”
> After will, use the base verb: “She will buy”, not “She will buys”.

## Lesson 24: Grammar- The past perfect tense

Prerequisites: 11, 15 · Skill: e2-24 (The Past Perfect tense)

**Note** (draft)

> We use the Past Perfect for an action that happened before another action in the past.
> Form: had + the past participle, the same for every person: I had finished, she had left, they had eaten.
> “When the ambulance arrived, the nurse had already stopped the bleeding.” (First the nurse stopped the bleeding; then the ambulance arrived.)
> Signal words: already, before, after, by the time, when.
> Negative: hadn't + participle: “We hadn't eaten.” Question: “Had you finished?” — “Yes, I had.”
> The later action is in the Simple Past: “By the time we got there, the meeting had begun.”

**Card c24-1** (draft)

> The Past Perfect is had + the past participle, for every person.
>

> _They had eaten lunch._

## Lesson 27: Grammar- Regular and irregular verbs

Prerequisites: 11, 15 · Skill: e2-27 (Regular and irregular verbs)

**Note** (draft)

> Every verb has three main forms: the base (go), the simple past (went) and the past participle (gone), used after have, has and had.
> Regular verbs add -ed for both: visit, visited, visited. Spelling: stop → stopped, carry → carried, arrive → arrived, travel → travelled.
> Irregular verbs change in their own way, and must be learnt:
> • all three the same: put, put, put; cut, cut, cut;
> • past = participle: buy, bought, bought; bring, brought, brought; leave, left, left;
> • all three different: go, went, gone; see, saw, seen; write, wrote, written; begin, began, begun; speak, spoke, spoken.
> Use the simple past alone (I saw), and the participle after have, has or had (I have seen, she had gone).

## Lesson 30: Vocabulary-Word formation

Prerequisites: none · Skill: e2-30 (Word formation: prefixes and suffixes)

**Note** (draft)

> We can make new words by adding a part at the beginning (a prefix) or at the end (a suffix).
> Prefixes change the meaning: un- (unhappy), dis- (disagree), im- (impossible, before m and p), in- (incorrect), re- (rewrite: write again), mis- (misunderstand: understand wrongly).
> Suffixes change the kind of word:
> • verb → noun: celebrate → celebration, govern → government, decorate → decoration, enjoy → enjoyment, sing → singer;
> • adjective → noun: happy → happiness, kind → kindness, free → freedom;
> • noun → adjective: care → careful (with care) or careless (without care), peace → peaceful, joy → joyful, nation → national;
> • adjective → adverb: quick → quickly, happy → happily.
> Spelling: y after a consonant becomes i (happy → happiness), and a final e often drops before -ion (celebrate → celebration).

## Lesson 31: Grammar-Modals and polite expression

Prerequisites: none · Skill: e2-31 (Modals and polite expressions)

**Note** (draft)

> Modal verbs go before a base verb and add a meaning:
> • can: ability or permission — “I can swim.” “Can I go out?”
> • could: a more polite request, or past ability — “Could you help me, please?” “I could read at five.”
> • may: polite permission or possibility — “May I come in?” “It may rain.”
> • must: strong obligation — “You must wear a uniform.” mustn't: it is forbidden.
> • should: advice — “You should rest.”
> • would: polite offers and requests — “Would you like some tea?” “Would you open the window, please?”
> After a modal, use the base verb with no to and no -s: “She can swim” (not “she cans” or “can to swim”).
> Polite expressions: please, thank you, excuse me, I'm sorry, “Would you mind …?”

**Card c31-1** (draft)
