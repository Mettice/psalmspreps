**Summary (the note)**

> Ancient Ghana (called Wagadu by its people, the Soninke) was a great empire of West Africa, in the area of today's south-east Mauritania and western Mali, not in the modern country of Ghana, which later took its name.
> It flourished from about the 8th century and was at its height in the 9th–11th centuries.
> The king had the title “Ghana” (war chief) and was also called Kaya Maghan, “lord of gold”. The capital, Koumbi Saleh, had two towns: one for the king and one for Muslim traders.
> Wealth came from the trans-Saharan trade: gold from the south (Bambuk, Bure) was exchanged for salt from the Sahara (Taghaza), carried by camel caravans. The king taxed goods entering and leaving and kept all gold nuggets.
> Decline: attacks by the Almoravids (who took Koumbi Saleh in 1076), drought, loss of trade, and the rise of Sosso and then Mali.

## Lesson 3: Migration waves in Cameroon

Prerequisites: 1 · Skill: y2-3 (Migration waves in Cameroon)

**Note** (draft)

> Cameroon's peoples came from several waves of migration over many centuries. Historians group them as:
> • Bantu peoples, mostly in the South and along the coast (Duala, Bakweri, Bassa, Beti, Bulu, Ewondo, Maka);
> • semi-Bantu (Grassfields) peoples in the western highlands (Bamileke, Tikar, Bamum, Bali, Nso, Mankon);
> • Sudanese peoples in the north (Mafa, Massa, Musgum, Fali, often called Kirdi), many of whom took refuge in the Mandara mountains;
> • the Fulani (Fulbe), cattle herders who arrived mainly in the 18th and 19th centuries and spread south during the jihad led by Modibo Adama (from 1809);
> • the Shuwa Arabs and Hausa traders in the north.
> Causes of migration: search for fertile land and pasture, wars and slave raids, the Fulani jihad, population pressure, famine and trade.

## Lesson 6: Generalities on state-like (structured) societies of Cameroon

## Lesson 9: Ghana :From the 9th to the 10th centuries

Prerequisites: none · Skill: y2-9 (Ancient Ghana from the 9th to the 10th centuries)

**Note** (draft)

> Ancient Ghana (called Wagadu by its people, the Soninke) was a great empire of West Africa, in the area of today's south-east Mauritania and western Mali, not in the modern country of Ghana, which later took its name.
> It flourished from about the 8th century and was at its height in the 9th–11th centuries.
> The king had the title “Ghana” (war chief) and was also called Kaya Maghan, “lord of gold”. The capital, Koumbi Saleh, had two towns: one for the king and one for Muslim traders.
> Wealth came from the trans-Saharan trade: gold from the south (Bambuk, Bure) was exchanged for salt from the Sahara (Taghaza), carried by camel caravans. The king taxed goods entering and leaving and kept all gold nuggets.
> Decline: attacks by the Almoravids (who took Koumbi Saleh in 1076), drought, loss of trade, and the rise of Sosso and then Mali.
