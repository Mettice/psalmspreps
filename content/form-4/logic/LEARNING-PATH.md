# Form Four Logic — outcomes and connections

Each opening Learn screen states the outcome, earlier knowledge and next use. These are teaching content, not an extra assessment gate.

| Row | Topic | By the end | Builds on | Prepares for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Structure of a proposition | separate quantifier, subject term, copula and predicate term in a standard categorical statement | Form Three’s distinction between a term, a judgment and an argument | categorical propositions, where these parts express a relation between classes |
| 2 | Categorical propositions | recognise a class-to-class assertion and explain what all, no and some commit it to | identifying the subject, predicate, copula and quantifier | quantity, quality and the standard categorical forms |
| 3 | Properties: quantity and quality | identify quantity and quality independently and explain which property changes in a rewrite | categorical claims and their quantifiers | combining the properties to recognise A, E, I and O |
| 4 | Standard categorical forms: A, E, I and O | classify and construct the standard forms and identify a counterexample to a universal claim | quantity and quality as separate properties | distribution and Venn diagrams, which depend on the exact form |
| 5 | Distribution of terms | state and justify which terms are distributed in each categorical form | the A, E, I and O forms | interpreting diagrams and avoiding illicit class-wide conclusions in later syllogisms |
| 6 | Symbolisation and Venn diagrams | translate between a categorical statement, its A/E/I/O symbol and a correctly marked Venn diagram | categorical forms and whole-class coverage | clear symbolisation before combining claims in later arguments |
| 8 | Hypothetical or conditional propositions | identify antecedent and consequent, preserve the direction of a condition and recognise its counterexample | a proposition as a truth-valued claim; categorical inclusion as a directed relation | disjunctions and the later symbolic-logic truth tables |
| 9 | Disjunctive propositions | distinguish inclusive from exclusive or, evaluate each and infer the remaining alternative when one is denied | compound propositions and truth-value evaluation in the conditional lesson | accurate translation and later truth tables for logical connectives |
| 10 | Other propositions and translation into standard form | translate only and not all accurately, recognise singular and indefinite cases, and preserve an exception | sentence parts, categorical forms, conditionals and disjunctions | Term Two’s translation into symbolic logic and later argument analysis |

Guided-work row 7 is excluded. The exact numbered prerequisites are also recorded in each lesson JSON. Goals are checked through identification, translation, computed cases and error analysis, rather than a completion claim based only on reading.

Broad destination: represent an ordinary claim accurately enough to analyse an argument without changing its scope, direction or existence assumptions.
