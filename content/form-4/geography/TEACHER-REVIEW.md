# Form Four Geography — Term One

All nineteen teaching rows are drafted with goals/connections, Learn, three cards, a worked example, five question templates and a summary. Each template has at least three real content variants and specific wrong-option feedback. The registry already connects the spine's “Geography Ok” header to Geography; row 10's missing teaching-unit metadata does not alter its sequence. No spine or registry change was needed.

- [ ] **TR-geography-1 — Coverage and examination depth.** Confirm terminology and expected explanatory depth across soil systems/properties/profiles, erosion/conservation, ecosystems, the six biome lessons and Cameroon physical geography. Examples and diagrams teach relationships and interpretation; they do not replace the approved atlas, textbook or extended examination practice. Further-term practical rows are outside this batch.
- [ ] **TR-geography-2 — Soil and profile conventions.** The profile is an original idealisation with O/A/B/C/R and an explanation that E can occur and horizons can be absent. C and R are distinguished as layers, with transported parent material possible. Confirm school usage of leaching/eluviation and soil-reaction conventions. Texture, structure, porosity and permeability are distinguished. Volume/mass exercises use supplied classroom models, not a universal soil mixture or a full texture classification.
- [ ] **TR-geography-3 — Erosion and conservation.** The original priority sketch holds soil/rain equal and compares stated slope and cover. A choice of the steepest bare plot is conditional on that simplified comparison, not a real farm recommendation. Cover, contour measures, windbreaks and terraces are explained by process. No digging, burning, chemical dose, drainage alteration or engineering design is assigned. Actual implementation requires site evidence and appropriate expertise.
- [ ] **TR-geography-4 — Ecosystem models.** Confirm food-arrow and nutrient-cycle terminology. Transfer percentages are explicit assumptions rather than a universal efficiency law. Energy is distinguished from recycled mineral nutrients; decomposer material comes from several trophic levels. The pond grid is fictional. No biodiversity count, water-quality claim or field collection is inferred from its location.
- [ ] **TR-geography-5 — Biome boundaries and calendars.** Rainforest, savannah, monsoon, hot desert and Mediterranean categories contain internal variation. The monsoon row distinguishes seasonal circulation from a storm and avoids copying an Asian calendar onto Cameroon. The Mediterranean chart explicitly uses the northern hemisphere. The Far North is not labelled uniformly hot desert. Check the prescribed biome names and locally accepted examples, including management effects and transition zones.
- [ ] **TR-geography-6 — Numerical and visual data.** All rainfall charts, erosion measurements, water/energy accounts and point coordinates are invented teaching data. Rainfall figures show monthly bars and calculated annual sums, not observed town climatologies. A rainfall chart alone does not establish a biome or wind reversal. Soil-profile and basin schematics are not to scale; the latter shows connections rather than river courses. Both coordinate exercises use existing computed Geography helpers.
- [ ] **TR-geography-7 — Cameroon location and relief.** Area is rounded to about 475,000 km² because published figures differ (BGS/AQUASTAT and UNData do not give the same exact total). Mount Cameroon is described as exceeding 4,000 m rather than forcing one exact summit figure. Confirm atlas/examination conventions for boundaries, area and peak heights. Six neighbouring states and the two Congo states are distinguished. The fictional graticule station is not a named town or a country-outline map.
- [ ] **TR-geography-8 — Drainage.** Confirm the school's basin-count convention, particularly separation of the Sanaga from other coastal systems. The schematic preserves Sanaga/Nyong–Atlantic, Benue–Niger, Dja/Sangha–Congo and Logone/Chari–Lake Chad connections. The Lake Chad basin is distinguished from normal ocean-outlet systems. Drawn connections do not imply navigability, constant discharge or safe water.
- [ ] **TR-geography-9 — Cameroon climate, vegetation and soils.** School climate labels and traditional soil classifications do not align perfectly with every modern scheme. Confirm coastal/Guinean/Cameroonian, Sudanian/Sahelian and highland terminology, and ferrallitic/ferruginous/volcanic/alluvial/hydromorphic/shrink-swell groupings. Original Bambouto/Bokito research demonstrates within-region variation. Avoid universal fertility claims or one-to-one colour/crop rules. Current vegetation is distinguished from potential vegetation and land-use history matters.
