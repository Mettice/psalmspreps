# Form Four Geography — learning path

Every opening Learn screen states the goal, earlier knowledge and next use. Numbered prerequisites record earlier lessons.

| Row | Topic | By the end | Builds on | Leads to |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Soil: components and an open system | identify soil components and balance air and water within a stated pore-space model | Form Three’s weathering and land-surface processes | soil properties, profiles and erosion |
| 2 | Soil characteristics | distinguish texture structure porosity and chemical reaction and calculate a missing particle fraction | soil components and pore contents | reading a soil profile and explaining erosion risk |
| 3 | The soil profile | interpret labelled soil horizons and distinguish a profile model from every real soil sequence | components, texture and structure | topsoil loss and conservation decisions |
| 4 | Soil erosion | distinguish detachment transport and deposition and explain how cover and runoff influence loss | soil properties and the profile | choosing measures that address erosion processes |
| 5 | Soil conservation | use a labelled slope-and-cover map to prioritise erosion control and explain how a measure works | erosion agents, runoff and soil properties | ecosystem links and later land-use evaluation |
| 6 | The ecosystem | identify interacting living and non-living components and locate a model habitat on a grid | soil as an open system and conservation | energy transfer and nutrient cycling |
| 7 | How ecosystems function | trace energy flow and nutrient cycling and calculate a transfer using an explicitly assumed efficiency | ecosystem components and interactions | biomes and responses to environmental change |
| 8 | The tropical rainforest biome | connect year-round moisture and warmth to forest structure and interpret a model rainfall chart | ecosystem functioning and nutrient cycling | comparison with seasonal savannah and monsoon landscapes |
| 9 | Tropical savannah: climate and vegetation | explain seasonal moisture stress and relate grass-tree structure to rainfall fire and land use | rainforest structure and the role of moisture | savannah soils, wildlife and human activity |
| 10 | Savannah soils, animal life and human activity | connect seasonal resources to soils wildlife and land use and balance a stated water store | savannah seasonality and soil properties | monsoon environments and Cameroon’s regional contrasts |
| 11 | The tropical monsoon biome | connect seasonal wind circulation and rainfall to seasonal forest and farming constraints | seasonality in savannah and rainforest comparisons | desert and Mediterranean contrasts and Cameroon climate types |
| 12 | The hot desert biome | explain aridity and adaptations and distinguish low rainfall from absence of life | seasonal moisture limitation and monsoon contrasts | Mediterranean drought and the drier parts of Cameroon |
| 13 | The Mediterranean biome | relate dry summers and wetter winters to plant adaptations and seasonal land-use constraints | hot-desert aridity and seasonal vegetation responses | comparing Cameroon’s environments with global biome patterns |
| 14 | Cameroon: location and size | describe Cameroon’s position and neighbours and read a coordinate on a regional graticule | Form Three’s coordinates and this term’s global biome comparisons | Cameroon’s relief, rivers and climatic regions |
| 15 | Relief of Cameroon | locate broad relief regions and distinguish elevation from relative height and gradient | Cameroon’s location and soil-erosion processes | drainage divides and the influence of altitude on climate |
| 16 | Drainage of Cameroon | trace major river-basin connections and explain the role of divides rather than assuming all rivers share one outlet | Cameroon’s relief and the meaning of slope | climate, vegetation and water-related land-use questions |
| 17 | Climatic types of Cameroon | compare coastal southern-interior highland and northern regimes and explain how location and relief modify them | global seasonal climates and Cameroon’s position, relief and drainage | regional vegetation and soil patterns |
| 18 | Vegetation of Cameroon | relate major vegetation formations to climate altitude drainage and human modification | biomes and Cameroon’s climatic contrasts | soil patterns and later ecosystem and land-use evaluation |
| 19 | Soil types in Cameroon | connect broad soil groups to formation and landscape position while evaluating suitability from observed properties | soil components, profiles and Cameroon’s climate and vegetation | later resource management and evidence-based land-use decisions |

All nineteen Term One teaching rows are included. The registry already maps the spine header Geography Ok to Geography; no spine or shared registry edit was needed. Missing teaching-unit metadata at row 10 does not change its sequence number.

Destination: explain connections between soil, water, living systems, climate and human activity, then apply them to evidence from Cameroon. Models support reasoning; an actual field or land-use decision needs local data and appropriate expertise.
