# Form Four Economics — learning path

Each opening Learn screen makes its goal, earlier knowledge and next use visible. Numbered prerequisites also record the links.

| Row | Topic | By the end | Builds on | Leads to |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Business units and the sole proprietor | explain sole ownership, distinguish revenue from profit and identify an ownership trade-off | Form Three’s production, resources and choice | partnerships, which share ownership, resources and decisions |
| 2 | Partnerships | explain shared ownership and calculate a partner’s agreed profit share | the sole proprietor’s finance and control limits | companies and the distinction between owners’ liability and business debts |
| 3 | Joint-stock and limited-liability companies: ownership | distinguish a company from its owners and calculate nominal issued share capital | ownership and liability in sole proprietorships and partnerships | company governance and the mix of equity and borrowing |
| 4 | Companies: governance, benefits and limits | distinguish ownership from management and evaluate benefits alongside governance costs | separate legal identity, shares and continuity | capital structure and the consequences of financing decisions |
| 5 | Guided comparison of business units | choose a defensible form for a fictional case and explain a trade-off rather than naming a universal winner | sole ownership, partnerships and company identity | cooperative study and the later locality business-planning case |
| 6 | Capital structure of companies | distinguish equity from debt and relate nominal issued, called and paid-up capital in a stated textbook model | shares, shareholder returns and company financing needs | the practical calculation of capital amounts |
| 7 | Practical preparation: capital calculations | calculate authorised, issued, called, paid-up and uncalled amounts from a labelled fictional record | the company-capital distinctions just learned | judging financing constraints and later growth plans |
| 8 | Cooperatives: purpose, ownership and control | explain common member needs and distinguish democratic control from capital-based voting | comparison of ownership, finance and control | types of cooperative and the use of a cooperative surplus |
| 9 | Cooperatives: types, surplus and limitations | identify the service supplied by different cooperatives and calculate an allocation under an explicit rule | member ownership and democratic control | public ownership and comparison of enterprise objectives |
| 10 | Public enterprises | distinguish government ownership from regulation and assess commercial and service objectives | private and cooperative ownership | nationalisation and privatisation as changes in ownership |
| 11 | Guided preparation: a business plan for a locality | justify a fictional business choice using demand, resources, ownership and a simple budget | ownership comparisons, financing and enterprise objectives | scale, growth and location decisions that test the plan further |
| 12 | Nationalisation of industries | explain an ownership transfer to the state and assess possible purposes and costs | public, private and mixed ownership | privatisation as a movement in the opposite ownership direction |
| 13 | Privatisation of industries | distinguish privatisation from liberalisation or commercialisation and evaluate competing consequences | nationalisation and mixed ownership | judging scale and growth without assuming ownership alone settles performance |
| 14 | Economies of scale | explain why larger scale can lower long-run average cost and calculate the saving per unit | resources, finance and the cost of supplying output | diseconomies of scale and the limits to expansion |
| 15 | Diseconomies of scale | identify scale-related coordination costs and calculate a rise in long-run average cost | economies of scale and average-cost comparisons | evaluating growth instead of assuming bigger is always better |
| 16 | Growth of firms: internal growth and measures | distinguish size measures, calculate growth and explain how internal expansion can be financed | scale economies, diseconomies and financing | external growth through mergers and acquisitions |
| 17 | Growth of firms: mergers, takeovers and integration | classify external growth by the firms’ relationship and evaluate a possible gain against a risk | internal growth and the benefits and limits of scale | why some firms remain small and how growth changes location needs |
| 18 | Small firms: roles, survival and limits | explain why a small firm may survive and calculate profit without confusing small size with failure | growth methods and scale advantages or disadvantages | location choices suited to the activity and its customers |
| 19 | Location of firms: production and market factors | explain a location factor’s effect on an activity and calculate a stated transport-cost difference | firm objectives, scale and specialised demand | comparing complete sites and the benefits or costs of industrial clusters |
| 20 | Location of firms: site choice, clusters and policy | compare complete site evidence and distinguish firm location from industrial concentration | input, market, labour and infrastructure factors | later production, cost and market analysis using evidence rather than one-factor rules |

All Term One rows 1–20 are included. Spine rows 5 and 11 are labelled lesson but titled guided work: these are fictional comparison/planning preparation. Row 7 is a capital-calculation practical taught from a labelled classroom record. No real enterprise must be formed and no money or private financial information is required.

Overall destination: explain how an enterprise’s ownership and objectives shape finance, scale, growth and location, then support a decision with evidence, calculations and recognised limitations.
