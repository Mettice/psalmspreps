# Form Four History — learning path

Every opening Learn screen states a measurable goal, earlier knowledge and next use. Prerequisite lesson numbers are also stored in JSON.

| Row | Topic | By the end | Builds on | Leads to |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Causes of the Second World War | distinguish underlying causes from the immediate European trigger and explain how aggression and failed restraint interacted | Form Three’s imperial rivalry, the First World War and its settlement | Cameroon’s involvement in a war fought through imperial networks |
| 2 | Cameroonians in the Second World War: reasons and roles | explain why colonial rule drew Cameroonians into the conflict and distinguish military, labour and material contributions | the causes of war and the division of Cameroon under British and French mandates | wartime effects in each administered territory |
| 3 | Effects of the war on the Southern British Cameroons | connect wartime economic disruption with postwar plantation change and stronger political demands without confusing their dates | Cameroon’s colonial mobilisation and contributions | comparison with French-administered Cameroon and postwar nationalism |
| 4 | Effects of the war on French-administered Cameroon | explain political openings and economic burdens and distinguish promised reform from continued colonial inequality | wartime contributions and the Southern British Cameroons comparison | Brazzaville and the rise of pressure groups |
| 5 | Africa’s participation in the Second World War | explain varied reasons for involvement and classify military and civilian roles using named regional examples | Cameroonian mobilisation and contrasting wartime effects | reform debates and the growth of African nationalism |
| 6 | The Brazzaville Conference: reasons and setting | explain why French leaders convened the conference and distinguish its colonial reform agenda from an independence congress | Africa’s wartime contributions and reform expectations | the recommendations and limits of Brazzaville |
| 7 | The Brazzaville Conference: recommendations and limits | classify political social and labour recommendations and explain why reform did not amount to a promise of independence | the reasons and official setting of Brazzaville | postwar nationalism and the trusteeship debate |
| 9 | African nationalism: meaning and forms | distinguish demands for collective self-government from a limited reform and explain how nationalism differed from Pan-Africanism | colonial rule, wartime change and the limits of Brazzaville | the internal and external factors strengthening nationalist movements |
| 10 | Factors promoting African nationalism | classify internal and external influences and explain how a grievance became organised pressure for political change | the meaning of nationalism and Pan-African solidarity | Cameroonian pressure groups and party formation |
| 11 | Origins of the Cold War | explain how the wartime alliance gave way to rivalry and distinguish ideological disagreement from postwar security disputes | the Second World War and the changing international setting of African nationalism | the Cold War’s policies, alliances and conflicts |
| 12 | The Cold War: causes and manifestations | connect containment, aid, alliances, arms competition and proxy conflicts to the rivalry without treating every local war as wholly foreign-made | the breakdown of the wartime alliance | détente and attempts to manage superpower competition |
| 13 | Détente: meaning and manifestations | recognise negotiated reductions in tension and distinguish arms limitation from disarmament or the end of the Cold War | Cold War rivalry, alliances and nuclear risk | assessing agreements and the continued choices of states during decolonisation |
| 14 | Cameroon under UN trusteeship: agreement and administration | explain why mandate territories entered trusteeship and distinguish the administering authority from UN supervision and petitions | mandate administration, postwar reform and international institutions | pressure groups using local organisation and international scrutiny |
| 15 | Pressure groups and political parties in French Cameroon | explain how labour and political networks developed and distinguish an organisation’s immediate demands from a party’s programme | wartime grievances, nationalist mobilisation and trusteeship channels | later study of party rivalry, independence and reunification |

Teaching rows 1–7 and 9–15 are included. Rows 8 (Algerian FLN) and 16 (UPC further study) are classified further_study and are outside the assigned lesson rows; brief UPC context still appears in row 15. The later chapter-numbering warning at row 40 does not alter this Term One sequence.

Destination: explain how wartime mobilisation, reform limits, international rivalry and new forums connected to organised demands for political change. Distinguish cause from trigger, proposal from implementation, and source claim from corroborated history.
