# Form Four English — learning path

Every opening Learn screen states the end goal, earlier knowledge and next use. Numbered prerequisites record the same dependencies.

| Row | Topic | By the end | Builds on | Leads to |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | Discussing care for family members and friends | offer and discuss respectful practical support without making assumptions about a person’s needs | Form Three’s polite requests and reasoned suggestions | family-life discussion and directed writing for an audience |
| 3 | Parts of speech and a tense overview | identify word functions in context and distinguish common tense/aspect patterns | Form Three’s sentence patterns and verb forms | choosing accurate simple forms, clause patterns and narrative tenses |
| 4 | Simple present and simple past | choose between a present habit and a completed past event and form a past negative correctly | parts of speech and the tense overview | transitivity, future forms and later narrative tense contrasts |
| 5 | Introduction to directed writing | produce a response that fulfils the specified purpose, audience, form and content points | sentence accuracy and familiar school communication | essay planning, letters and later talks or reports |
| 6 | Written preparation: studying-skills listening script | extract the main idea and supporting details from an original study-skills script | directed writing and organising task points | later listening practice and evidence-based reading |
| 7 | Written preparation: /θ/ and /ð/ | group written model words by their initial consonant and explain the stated voicing distinction | written sound-symbol preparation and careful dictionary use | clearer word recognition and later teacher-led pronunciation practice |
| 8 | Essay planning, thesis statements and topic sentences | plan a focused essay with a controlling idea and paragraphs that develop it | directed-writing purpose and sentence accuracy | argumentative and narrative writing with an organised structure |
| 9 | Discussing aspects of family life | exchange views about shared responsibilities, respect and communication using fictional situations | polite offers, directed purpose and careful word choice | reading an original family-life passage and writing to different audiences |
| 10 | Reading an original family-life passage | identify a problem, proposed response and limits of the evidence in a family-life passage | respectful discussion and shared-responsibility language | letters, argument and clear reports of information |
| 11 | Transitive and intransitive verb uses | identify a direct object and distinguish transitive from intransitive uses in context | word functions and finite clause patterns | adverbials, clause structure and accurate sentence construction |
| 12 | Adverbs of frequency, place and manner | identify what an adverb contributes and choose a suitable position in a clear model sentence | word classes, verb uses and adverbial phrases | precise description and narrative detail |
| 13 | Formal and informal letters | write a letter whose layout, tone and content fit its reader and purpose | directed-writing instructions and paragraph planning | talks, reports and controlled changes of register |
| 14 | Discussing club and association activities | express a club preference with reasons, ask for clarification and propose a shared activity | family-life discussion, adverbs and letter purpose | future plans and argumentative writing about school participation |
| 15 | Future reference with will and other forms | form will plus a base verb and choose a future expression suited to its context | the simple present/past contrast and auxiliary patterns | conditional sentences about possible future situations |
| 16 | Context clues and collocation | infer a supported word meaning from context and choose a natural word combination | word classes and sentence meaning | careful reading of trade passages and precise writing |
| 17 | Conditional sentences | match a common conditional pattern to its meaning and correct a malformed clause | past, present and future-reference forms | reasoned argument and later imagined or past situations |
| 18 | Prepositions of time, place, purpose and direction | choose a preposition or related purpose construction from the meaning of the whole phrase | adverbials and clause relationships | accurate descriptions, reports and movement narratives |
| 19 | Written preparation: /tʃ/ and /dʒ/ | group written model words by their initial consonant and explain the stated voicing distinction | written sound-symbol preparation and careful dictionary use | clearer word recognition and later teacher-led pronunciation practice |
| 20 | Reading: e-commerce and face-to-face trade | compare the passage’s trading arrangements and interpret its vocabulary without assuming unreported prices or outcomes | context clues, collocations and reasoned comparison | argumentative writing and checking claims about transactions |
| 21 | Sentence types and communicative purpose | distinguish declarative, interrogative, imperative and exclamative forms without relying on punctuation alone | verb and clause patterns in statements and questions | argumentative writing and sentence-structure analysis |
| 22 | Argumentative essays | develop a position with reasons, relevant support and a fair response to a counterargument | essay focus, contextual evidence and conditional reasoning | stronger paragraph structure and balanced formal argument |
| 23 | Simple, compound, complex and compound-complex sentences | classify sentence structure by independent and dependent clauses and repair a comma splice | sentence types, conjunctions and finite clause patterns | organised narratives and varied formal writing |
| 24 | Homonyms, homophones and homographs | distinguish sound, spelling and meaning while recognising that terminology can overlap | context clues, word classes and written sound symbols | editing narrative vocabulary and avoiding wrong-word spellings |
| 25 | Narrative essays | write a coherent original narrative with setting, linked events and a meaningful ending | paragraph planning, sentence structure and simple-past events | past-perfect chronology and later report/narrative contrasts |
| 26 | Numbers, figures, words and currency names | match a number phrase to its figure and name the currencies in a checked country list | word meaning, written forms and place-value knowledge | a written money script and precise quantities in reports |
| 27 | Written preparation: a money-and-amount listening script | extract an amount and transaction sequence from a written script before later listening practice | figures, number words and currency labels | clear numerical references in talks and reports |
| 28 | Writing a talk for an audience | prepare a talk with an appropriate address, clear message, supporting points and a purposeful ending | directed writing, letters and argumentative paragraph organisation | spoken delivery practice and later reports for different audiences |
| 29 | Simple past and past perfect | show the order of past events with simple past and had plus a participle | simple past and the overview of perfect forms | clear chronology in narratives and reports |
| 30 | Discussing environmental protection | propose a realistic environmental action, explain its purpose and recognise a limitation | reasoned discussion, future expressions and talks for an audience | word-building, reports and evidence-based hygiene/safety reading |
| 31 | Prefixes, suffixes, synonyms and antonyms | use a word’s structure and context to choose a related form or meaning | word classes, contextual clues and homonym distinctions | editing reports and building precise vocabulary |
| 32 | Writing a factual report | organise findings and recommendations while separating observation, inference and proposal | narrative chronology, audience and formal-purpose writing | later evidence-based summaries and reports |
| 33 | Reading: hygiene and safety | use a passage to distinguish an authorised plan, a precaution and a completed result | environmental discussion and factual report writing | careful use of instructions and later health/safety vocabulary |
| 34 | Compound nouns and collocation | recognise a compound noun, choose a conventional form and preserve the relevant head in a plural | word structure, nouns and natural combinations | later formal writing and dictionary-based vocabulary extension |

Teaching rows 2–34 are included; row 1 is diagnostic/evaluation and excluded. Row 19 uses /tʃ/ and /dʒ/ as confirmed by Dion. Oral/listening rows offer written preparation and require a separate teacher/audio activity to assess speech or hearing.

Overall destination: interpret and produce purposeful English with accurate sentences, evidence-based reading, organised writing and a clear sense of audience. The missing Term Three spine header does not change the supplied Term One rows.
