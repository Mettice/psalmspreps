## Lesson 2: continues : Case studies of decolonization in Africa :

Prerequisites: 1 · Skill: y5-2 (Decolonisation case study: Nigeria’s negotiated transition)

**Note** (draft)

> Nigeria’s transition involved nationalist parties, regional interests, constitutional conferences and federal arrangements under British rule. A federal framework allocated authority between central and regional institutions; it did not mean that all political interests became identical. The federal election of 1959 preceded independence on 1 October 1960. Abubakar Tafawa Balewa headed the federal government at independence. Later instability should not be read backward as proof that constitutional negotiation had no meaning or that conflict was inevitable.

**Learn: Your goal and connections** (draft)

> By the end, you should be able to explain how federal arrangements and political negotiation shaped Nigeria’s independence. Builds on: Ghana’s constitutional route and the distinction between autonomy and sovereignty. Leads to: French West Africa and comparison of varied transitions.

**Learn: Explain the history** (draft)

> Nigeria’s transition involved nationalist parties, regional interests, constitutional conferences and federal arrangements under British rule. A federal framework allocated authority between central and regional institutions; it did not mean that all political interests became identical. The federal election of 1959 preceded independence on 1 October 1960. Abubakar Tafawa Balewa headed the federal government at independence. Later instability should not be read backward as proof that constitutional negotiation had no meaning or that conflict was inevitable.

**Learn: Follow the evidence and examples** (draft)

> Federal arrangements developed during the 1950s. The 1958 constitutional conference set out a path toward independence, followed by the 1959 election and parliamentary steps. Independence changed Nigeria’s status while questions of regional balance and political inclusion remained.
