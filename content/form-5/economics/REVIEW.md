## Lesson 2: Fundamental notions of money (Part 2)

Prerequisites: 1 · Skill: n5-2 (Money: qualities, forms and acceptance)

**Note** (draft)

> Useful money is generally acceptable, recognisable, durable, portable and divisible into workable units. These qualities help it perform functions but do not alone make every object money. Commodity money has a non-monetary commodity use; fiat currency does not depend on an equivalent commodity content. Banknotes and coins differ from transferable bank deposits. A card or cheque may instruct payment using a balance without being that balance itself. This is an economic distinction, not a guide to opening accounts or identifying counterfeits.

**Learn: Your goal and connections** (draft)

> By the end, you should be able to compare useful qualities of money and distinguish cash, deposits and payment instruments. Builds on: money’s functions and barter. Leads to: money demand, supply and banking.

**Learn: Understand the economics** (draft)

> Useful money is generally acceptable, recognisable, durable, portable and divisible into workable units. These qualities help it perform functions but do not alone make every object money. Commodity money has a non-monetary commodity use; fiat currency does not depend on an equivalent commodity content. Banknotes and coins differ from transferable bank deposits. A card or cheque may instruct payment using a balance without being that balance itself. This is an economic distinction, not a guide to opening accounts or identifying counterfeits.

**Learn: Follow the local case** (draft)

> A fictional market compares a bulky perishable crop with an accepted currency for small payments. Divisibility and durability can help explain the choice. In Cameroon the BEAC issues the common Central African CFA currency; deposit balances at commercial banks are a different form of monetary claim. A payment card accesses an account rather than storing every payment as physical notes.

**Learn: Reason and check** (draft)

> Separate a quality from a function and a form from its payment instrument. Explain why a quality helps exchange without assuming that it guarantees acceptance everywhere. Ask which institution owes or issues the monetary claim.

**Learn: key words and examples** (draft)

- **divisibility**: the ability to use practical smaller payment units
- **durability**: the ability to remain usable through ordinary handling over time
- **fiat currency**: currency whose monetary use does not require equivalent commodity content
- **deposit money**: a transferable monetary claim on a bank


Common mistake: “Divisibility means every good must be cut physically.” → “divisibility: the ability to use practical smaller payment units.”. The payment units, not necessarily goods, are divided.

## Lesson 8: Deflation

**Note** (draft)

## Lesson 10: Commercial Bank

**Note** (draft)

> Commercial banks provide customer deposits, payments and credit, normally subject to banking rules and commercial constraints. ## Lesson 16: The central bank

Prerequisites: 7, 9, 13, 15 · Skill: n5-16 (The central bank)

**Note** (draft)

> A central bank has system-wide monetary responsibilities, distinct from an ordinary commercial bank’s customer business. It can conduct monetary policy, issue currency under its framework and support settlement arrangements. Central-bank reserves used between banks differ from customer deposit balances. Policy influences monetary and credit conditions rather than producing goods directly. Government taxation and expenditure are fiscal decisions, not the same activity as monetary policy. > Public finance concerns government revenue, expenditure and financing. Taxes and non-tax receipts can provide revenue; borrowing finances a gap but is not counted as revenue in this stated model. Expenditure purchases services or assets and makes transfers under the applicable budget classification. Revenue minus expenditure gives a surplus if positive, a deficit if negative and balance if equal. A deficit is a flow during a period; public debt is a stock at a date. Fiscal choices differ from central-bank monetary policy.

**Learn: key words and examples** (draft)

- **public revenue**: tax and non-tax receipts included under the stated classification
- **public expenditure**: spending included under the stated public-budget classification
- **budget balance**: revenue less expenditure for the stated period
- **stock-flow distinction**: separation of a debt position from a period’s deficit
