# Form Five Economics — learning path

Every opening states the goal, earlier knowledge and next use. Amounts and ratios are computed; all classroom financial records are invented.

| Row | Topic | By the end | Builds on | Leads to |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Money: functions and the limits of barter | distinguish money’s functions and explain how a medium of exchange reduces a barter obstacle | Form Four trade, production and exchange | qualities of money, money demand and purchasing power |
| 2 | Money: qualities, forms and acceptance | compare useful qualities of money and distinguish cash, deposits and payment instruments | money’s functions and barter | money demand, supply and banking |
| 3 | Demand for and supply of money | explain why balances are held and distinguish a money stock from income flows | money forms and functions | value of money, inflation and bank deposit creation |
| 4 | The value of money | distinguish internal purchasing power from external exchange value and calculate a reciprocal index | money stock, transactions and comparable units | basket practical and inflation measurement |
| 5 | Practical preparation: a fixed-basket record | record comparable quantities and costs and explain what a small basket can establish | internal purchasing power and price indices | inflation measurement and careful comparisons |
| 6 | Inflation: meaning and measurement | calculate a price-index change and distinguish general inflation from a single price movement | basket records, indices and purchasing power | causes, effects and the distinction from deflation |
| 7 | Inflation: causes, effects and policy choices | connect a stated inflation pressure to a mechanism and explain a conditional effect | price-index changes and nominal versus real value | deflation and central-bank policy |
| 8 | Deflation and disinflation | distinguish a falling price level from a slowing rise and explain a possible consequence | inflation rates, purchasing power and nominal debt | banking and the monetary-policy role |
| 9 | Fundamental notions of banking | explain deposit-taking, payments and lending while distinguishing liquidity and credit risk | money forms, desired balances and real value | commercial-bank functions and the bank balance sheet |
| 10 | Commercial banks: functions and payments | distinguish moving existing deposits from creating a deposit through lending | bank claims, payment services and risk | balance-sheet entries and credit creation |
| 12 | A commercial bank’s balance sheet | classify bank assets and liabilities and reconcile them with equity | commercial-bank deposits, lending and risk | paired credit-creation entries and simplified expansion models |
| 13 | Credit creation: the loan and deposit entry | trace a new bank loan and matching deposit without confusing money creation with wealth creation | bank assets, liabilities and equity | the assumptions of the simplified deposit-expansion model |
| 14 | Credit creation: a simplified expansion model and its limits | calculate a model deposit total and separate its assumptions from real bank lending | paired loan/deposit entries and bank risk | guided model comparison in a Cameroon setting |
| 15 | Practical preparation: credit-creation records in Cameroon | construct a finite deposit-round record and identify why it is not an observed lending total | loan/deposit entries and the stylised multiplier | BEAC, central-bank functions and evidence-based interpretation |
| 16 | The central bank | distinguish monetary-policy and currency functions from commercial banking and fiscal decisions | money, bank settlement and credit constraints | a guided case study of BEAC and public finance |
| 17 | Guided case study: BEAC as a central bank | use an official institutional description to assign roles and avoid unsupported policy claims | central-bank and fiscal distinctions | public-finance foundations and later monetary analysis |
| 19 | Fundamental notions of public finance | distinguish public revenue, expenditure and financing and calculate a stated budget balance | government/central-bank distinctions and financial records | later taxation, public spending and budget-policy analysis |

Fourteen lesson rows plus practicals 5/15 and guided work 17 are drafted. Further-study rows 11 (cheques) and 18 (other institutions) are not lesson/practical rows and are excluded under the authoring row policy; integration/evaluation are absent here.

Overall destination: explain monetary and public-finance relationships, calculate stated records accurately and separate a classroom model from an institutional fact or real outcome.
