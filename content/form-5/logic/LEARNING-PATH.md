# Form Five Logic — learning path

Each opening states a measurable goal and its connections.

| Row | Topic | By the end | Builds on | Leads to |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Arguments and reasoning | distinguish an offered inference from description or explanation and assess its intended strength | Form Four statements, conditions and logical form | identifying premises, conclusions and indicators |
| 2 | Parts of an argument | extract complete premises and conclusion while separating background and inference | arguments and intended support | indicators and later analysis of a whole passage |
| 3 | Premise and conclusion indicators | interpret indicator expressions by their role in context rather than spotting words mechanically | argument parts and complete claims | opposition and reconstruction of reasoning |
| 4 | Opposition as immediate inference | identify opposition relations without changing the subject, predicate or existence assumptions | argument parts and Form Four categorical forms | using the square to infer truth values |
| 5 | Squares of opposition | use opposition and subalternation while distinguishing false from undetermined | categorical meanings and opposition relations | guided square problems and eduction |
| 6 | Guided work: checking a square-of-opposition record | construct a complete counted model and use it to detect an unjustified square inference | square relations and existence conditions | conversion, obversion and checking transformations |
| 7 | Eduction and simple conversion | transform a categorical claim by a licensed operation and reject an unsupported converse | opposition, complete terms and counterexample models | obversion and chains of immediate inference |
| 8 | Obversion | obvert each categorical form by changing quality and complementing the whole predicate | categorical forms, conversion and fixed terms | complex eduction without changing a claim’s scope |
| 9 | Complex modes of eduction | justify a chain of licensed transformations and distinguish contraposition from a converse | simple conversion, obversion and existence conditions | analysing complete passages without changing their claims |
| 10 | Identifying argument parts in a passage | reconstruct a qualified conclusion and its supporting reason without adding a stronger claim | argument roles, indicators and transformation limits | later assessment of syllogisms and argumentative prose |

Rows 1–10 include nine teaching rows and guided work 6, which is possible on screen. No diagnostic is listed.

Overall destination: reconstruct an argument faithfully and test immediate inferences without changing terms, scope or existence assumptions.
