## Lesson 2: European powers on the coast of Cameroon before annexation

Prerequisites: none · Skill: y3-2 (European powers on the Cameroon coast before annexation)

**Note** (draft)

> Overseas contact developed before formal colonial rule. Portuguese arrivals were recorded on the coast in the fifteenth century; later British, German and other trading connections grew. Merchants sought products and access, while missions pursued religious and educational activities. African traders and rulers negotiated these relationships and protected their own interests. Coastal commerce was not the same thing as possession of the whole interior. The existence of a European ship or mission station does not establish that all local communities had surrendered sovereignty.

**Learn: Understand the ideas** (draft)

> Overseas contact developed before formal colonial rule. Portuguese arrivals were recorded on the coast in the fifteenth century; later British, German and other trading connections grew. Merchants sought products and access, while missions pursued religious and educational activities. African traders and rulers negotiated these relationships and protected their own interests. Coastal commerce was not the same thing as possession of the whole interior. The existence of a European ship or mission station does not establish that all local communities had surrendered sovereignty.

**Learn: Apply the ideas locally** (draft)

> Duala traders acted between inland producers and overseas firms. British commercial and missionary ties were established before the German claim in 1884. German firms also built interests on the coast. Comparing their roles helps explain later rivalry, but European records must be read alongside evidence of African choices and authority.

**Learn: Reason and check** (draft)

> Distinguish contact, trade, mission work and formal political control. Ask what area a source actually describes. Do not use the colonial language of discovery to imply that land was unknown to its inhabitants. Identify the interests and standpoint of the person writing the record.

**Learn: key words and examples** (draft)

- **commercial contact**: overseas exchange before formal political annexation
- **mission activity**: religious work that could include schooling and translation
- **African agency**: local actors pursuing their own aims in relationships
- **limited coastal reach**: the distinction between coastal influence and control of the interior

- A merchant exchanges goods with coastal trading intermediaries.
- A mission establishes a congregation and teaches reading.
- A coastal ruler negotiates conditions to protect trade and land.

Common mistake: “Every commercial exchange transfers sovereignty over the whole interior.” → “commercial contact: overseas exchange before formal political annexation.”. Trade and a transfer of political authority are different relationships.

## Lesson 10: Reaction of the natives towards German annexation- Case studies, Douala war/Land case, Bakweri, Mamfe, Bangwa, Nso etc

Prerequisites: none · Skill: y3-10 (Responses to German rule: resistance and protest)

**Note** (draft)

> Resistance to German expansion included armed defence, petitions and refusal of imposed demands. Bakweri, Bangwa, Mamfe/Cross River and Nso campaigns had distinct causes and sequences. Early coastal conflict must also be separated from the later Duala land protest associated with Rudolf Duala Manga Bell. Compare demand, response and consequence, keeping African decisions visible. An official label such as rebellion does not prove that a community had freely accepted colonial authority. A military defeat did not erase continuing opposition or the costs of colonial rule.

**Learn: Coastal and Bakweri responses** (draft)

> Lock Priso’s opposition in 1884 differed from later Duala land petitions. Rudolf Duala Manga Bell challenged expropriation and was executed in 1914. Around Buea, Kuva Likenye defended Bakweri authority and land against intrusion. His forces repelled the German attack in 1891; a better-armed attack in 1894 defeated them. An early victory did not ensure permanent independence.

**Learn: Bangwa: recruitment and changing relations** (draft)

> Asunganyi initially received Gustav Conrau, who recruited workers for coastal plantations in 1898. When Conrau sought more workers without the earlier recruits returning, relations deteriorated. Conrau was detained and died after attempting escape; accounts differ about the precise circumstances. Bangwa resistance met repeated German raids, destruction and seizures. Asunganyi continued opposition before his capture and exile in 1911. Initial cooperation did not mean unconditional consent.

**Learn: Mamfe and the Upper Cross River** (draft)

> Colonial intrusion, coercion and commercial demands provoked opposition among communities around the Cross River. In 1904, Pückler-Limpurg’s expedition met armed resistance; he died during the fighting. Attacks also targeted trading posts. German forces retaliated through destructive campaigns, including burning settlements and crops and executing opponents. Colonial control followed severe repression. Mamfe is a regional label here, not proof that every community had one ruler or identical aims.

**Learn: Nso: distinguish the encounters** (draft)

> Nso defended its authority amid unequal colonial demands. Pavel’s armed presence must be distinguished from Houben’s attack in 1902 and the further campaign in 1906, after which German control and tribute demands were imposed. The palace suffered burning and looting during military violence. Ngonnso’s removal has a contested acquisition history: do not assign it to a particular battle without evidence. Compare local memory and colonial records, then explain each campaign’s causes, methods and consequences.

## Lesson 12: Effects and weaknesses of the Berlin West African conference on the rest of Africa

**Note** (draft)

**Learn: Understand the ideas** (draft)

## Lesson 13: Methods of Acquisition of African territories by European powers: Annexation, concession, protectorate, Negotiation, Military Conquest, Mandate

Prerequisites: none · Skill: y3-13 (Methods of acquiring colonial territory)

**Note** (draft)

> Imperial acquisition used treaties, protectorate claims, annexation, concessions and military conquest, often in combination. A concession granted specified rights, not automatically full sovereignty over a whole region. A protectorate claim asserted outside control under a language of protection; annexation claimed incorporation. Military conquest imposed control through force. Mandates belonged to the post-First World War framework and should not be placed before the Cameroon claim in 1884. The historical label does not settle the legitimacy or fairness of the process.

**Learn: Understand the ideas** (draft)

**Learn: Apply the ideas locally** (draft)

**Learn: Reason and check** (draft)
