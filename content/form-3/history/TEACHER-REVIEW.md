# Form Three History — Term One

Draft rows 1–13 and 15–19; guided-work row 14 excluded. Every row has Learn and five questions with four content variants in the concept banks.

- [ ] **TR-history-1 — Precolonial diversity and agency.** Confirm local examples and terminology. The territory of modern Cameroon is not treated as one precolonial state, and African knowledge, negotiation and disagreement are retained in accounts of overseas contact.
- [ ] **TR-history-2 — Annexation sequence.** Verify the conventional school dates for the German-Duala treaty and flag ceremony, the parties, Nachtigal/Hewett roles and the geographic limits of coastal claims. Documents and secondary accounts differ in how they describe individual agreements; the lesson distinguishes claims from effective control and unanimous consent.
- [ ] **TR-history-3 — Berlin framework.** Check the dates, participating powers, notification/effective-occupation provisions, trade/navigation and African exclusion. The conference is not said to have drawn every present boundary or to have caused the earlier coastal treaty.
- [ ] **TR-history-4 — Regional resistance cases.** Row 10 now includes named Bakweri, Bangwa, Mamfe/Cross River and Nso case outlines with causes, methods, sequence and consequences. Verify their depth against the approved class text. Check spellings Asunganyi/Asonganyi and Pückler-Limpurg, the regional use of Mamfe, and the distinct Nso encounters in 1902/1906. The circumstances of Conrau's death and Ngonnso's removal are not asserted as settled; do not collapse Pavel's presence, Houben's attack and the later campaign into one event. No exact casualty totals are invented. Keep early coastal conflict distinct from later Duala land protest and Rudolf Duala Manga Bell's execution.
- [ ] **TR-history-5 — Colonial development and policy labels.** Verify direct/indirect rule and assimilation/association distinctions and the evidence for local economic/social change. Material facilities are evaluated alongside extraction, coercion and unequal access. No colonial actor's claim is treated as evidence of African popular consent.
- [ ] **TR-history-6 — Adwa.** Verify Menelik II, Taytu, the Wuchale dispute and the consequences of the victory. The lesson does not erase later invasion or treat commemorative art as a literal eyewitness battle map.
