**Note** (draft)

> OHADA (Organisation for the Harmonisation of Business Law in Africa) was created by a treaty signed in Port Louis in 1993; Cameroon is a member. Its accounting law, SYSCOHADA (revised in 2017), gives member states the same accounting rules.
> The chart of accounts groups accounts in classes by their first digit:
> 1 equity and long-term liabilities (101 Capital, 162 Loans) · 2 fixed assets (231 Buildings, 245 Transport equipment) · 3 stocks (311 Goods) · 4 third parties (401 Suppliers, 411 Customers, 443 VAT invoiced) · 5 treasury (521 Bank, 571 Cash) · 6 expenses (601 Purchases of goods, 661 Salaries) · 7 income (701 Sales of goods) · 8 other income and expenses.

**Learn: OHADA and SYSCOHADA** (draft)

> OHADA is the Organisation for the Harmonisation of Business Law in Africa. Its treaty was signed in Port Louis in 1993, and Cameroon is one of its member states.
> OHADA makes 'uniform acts' that apply in all member states. The accounting one is SYSCOHADA (revised in 2017): the same accounts and rules from Douala to Dakar, so accounts can be understood across borders.

**Learn: The chart of accounts** (draft)

> Each account has a number; its first digit gives its class:
> • 1 equity and long-term liabilities (Capital, Loans)
> • 2 fixed assets (Buildings, Equipment, Vehicles)
> • 3 stocks (Goods, Raw materials)
> • 4 third parties (Suppliers, Customers, Staff, State/VAT)
> • 5 treasury (Bank, Cash)
> • 6 expenses (Purchases, Salaries, Electricity)
> • 7 income (Sales, Services)
> • 8 other income and expenses.

**Learn: Enterprises and double entry** (draft)

> Enterprises may be sole traders, partnerships or companies (SARL, SA), working in trade, industry or services.
> Every transaction has two sides. In double entry each transaction is recorded in at least two accounts: one debited and one credited for the same amount. So total debits always equal total credits.
> A cash sale: debit Cash (money comes in), credit Sales (income earned).

**Learn: key words and examples** (draft)

- **OHADA**: organisation harmonising business law in member African states
- **SYSCOHADA**: the OHADA accounting system
- **chart of accounts**: the numbered list of accounts grouped in classes
- **double entry**: recording each transaction as an equal debit and credit

- 101 Capital: class 1
- 601 Purchases of goods: class 6
- 411 Customers: class 4

Common mistake: “Sales (701) is an expense account” → “Sales is an income account in class 7”. Class 6 holds expenses; class 7 holds income.

## Lesson 2: Journal, Ledger and Trial Balance

Prerequisites: 1 · Skill: a3-2 (The accounting cycle: journal, ledger and trial balance)

**Note** (draft)

> Accounting cycle: source documents (invoices, receipts) → journal → ledger → trial balance → financial statements.
> • Journal: transactions recorded in date order, each with the accounts debited and credited, amounts and a short explanation.
> • Ledger: each account on its own page (T-account); journal amounts are posted to the debit or credit side.
> • Balance of an account = difference between its debit total and credit total (debit balance if debits are bigger).
> • Trial balance: a list of all account balances; total debit balances must equal total credit balances. If not, there is an error.

**Learn: From documents to statements** (draft)

> Every transaction starts with a source document: an invoice, a receipt, a bank statement. The accounting cycle then follows:
> source documents → journal → ledger → trial balance → financial statements (balance sheet and income statement).

**Learn: Journal and ledger** (draft)

> The journal records transactions in date order. Each entry gives the date, the account(s) debited and credited, the amounts and a short description; the debits equal the credits.
> The ledger has one page (a T-account) for each account. Posting means copying each journal amount to the same side of the right ledger account.
> The balance of an account is the difference between its two sides: a debit balance if the debits are bigger, a credit balance if the credits are bigger.

**Learn: Trial balance** (draft)

> A trial balance lists every account with its balance, debit balances in one column and credit balances in the other. The two totals must be equal.
> If they differ, there is an error (a one-sided posting, a wrong addition). But equal totals do not prove everything is right: an entry left out completely, or posted to the wrong account on the correct side, does not show up.

**Learn: key words and examples** (draft)

- **journal**: book of first entry, in date order
- **ledger**: book of accounts, one page per account
- **posting**: copying journal amounts to the ledger
- **trial balance**: list of balances to check that debits equal credits

- Debits 500 000, credits 120 000: debit balance 380 000
- Trial balance totals 950 000 = 950 000: agrees

Common mistake: “Balance = debits + credits” → “Balance = the larger side − the smaller side”. A balance is the difference between the two sides.

Prerequisites: 2 · Skill: a3-3 (Value added tax (VAT): calculation and accounting entries)

**Note** (draft)

> VAT in Cameroon is 19.25% of the price excluding tax (HT): 17.5% VAT plus 10% of that as council tax (17.5% × 1.1 = 19.25%).
> • Amount including tax (TTC) = HT + VAT = HT × 1.1925.
> • On sales, the enterprise charges VAT: credit 443 VAT invoiced (collected).
> • On purchases, it pays VAT it can recover: debit 445 VAT recoverable.
> • VAT payable to the State = VAT invoiced − VAT recoverable (account 4441). If recoverable is bigger, the difference is a VAT credit (4449), carried forward.

**Learn: A tax on added value** (draft)

> Value added tax (VAT) is a tax on consumption. Each enterprise charges VAT on its sales and deducts the VAT it paid on its purchases, so in the end the final consumer bears the tax.
> In Cameroon the rate is 19.25%: 17.5% VAT plus council tax of 10% of it (17.5% × 1.1 = 19.25%).

**Learn: Calculating** (draft)

> • HT (hors taxes): price excluding tax.
> • VAT = 19.25% × HT.
> • TTC (toutes taxes comprises): HT + VAT = HT × 1.1925.
> For 200 000 FCFA HT: VAT = 38 500 FCFA and TTC = 238 500 FCFA.
> To go back from TTC to HT, divide by 1.1925.

**Learn: Recording and paying** (draft)

> • Sales: debit 411 Customers with TTC; credit 701 Sales with HT and 443 VAT invoiced with the VAT.
> • Purchases: debit 601 Purchases with HT and 445 VAT recoverable with the VAT; credit 401 Suppliers with TTC.
> • At the end of the month, VAT payable (4441) = VAT invoiced − VAT recoverable, paid to the State. If recoverable is bigger, the difference is a VAT credit (4449) carried to the next month.

**Learn: key words and examples** (draft)

- **VAT**: value added tax, 19.25% in Cameroon
- **HT**: amount excluding tax
- **TTC**: amount including all taxes
- **VAT credit**: recoverable VAT larger than invoiced VAT, carried forward

- HT 80 000: VAT 15 400, TTC 95 400
- Invoiced 50 000 − recoverable 30 000 = 20 000 to pay

Common mistake: “VAT = 19.25% of the TTC amount” → “VAT = 19.25% of the HT amount”. VAT is calculated on the price before tax.

## Lesson 4: Accounting for Financing Transactions (Capital and Loans Acquired)

Prerequisites: 1, 2 · Skill: a3-4 (Accounting for financing transactions: capital and loans)

**Note** (draft)

> Financing transactions bring long-term money into the enterprise.
> • Capital contributed by the owner: debit 521 Bank (or 571 Cash, or the asset given), credit 101 Capital.
> • Loan received from a bank: debit 521 Bank, credit 162 Loans from credit institutions.
> • Repayment of the loan: debit 162 Loans, credit 521 Bank. Interest paid is an expense: debit 671 Interest, credit 521 Bank.
> Capital and loans are resources (class 1); the money received is in treasury (class 5).

**Learn: Where the money comes from** (draft)

> A new business needs money to start. The two main long-term sources are the owner's capital and loans.
> Both are resources recorded in class 1: 101 Capital for what the owner puts in, 162 Loans from credit institutions for money borrowed from a bank.

**Learn: The entries** (draft)

> • Capital paid into the bank: debit 521 Bank, credit 101 Capital. Paid in cash: debit 571 Cash. If the owner brings a car, debit 245 Transport equipment.
> • Loan received: debit 521 Bank, credit 162 Loans.
> • Loan repaid: debit 162 Loans, credit 521 Bank.
> • Interest paid: debit 671 Interest (an expense), credit 521 Bank.

**Learn: Following the bank balance** (draft)

> Each financing transaction changes the bank balance: capital and loans add to it; repayments and interest take from it.
> Capital 2 000 000 + loan 1 500 000 − repayment 300 000 leaves 3 200 000 FCFA in the bank, and the loan account shows 1 200 000 FCFA still owed.

**Learn: key words and examples** (draft)

- **capital**: money or assets contributed by the owner
- **loan**: money borrowed that must be repaid
- **interest**: the cost of borrowing, an expense
- **financing transaction**: a transaction that brings long-term funds

- Dr 521 Bank / Cr 101 Capital
- Dr 521 Bank / Cr 162 Loans
- Dr 671 Interest / Cr 521 Bank

Common mistake: “A loan is credited to Capital” → “A loan is credited to 162 Loans”. The loan belongs to the bank and must be repaid; capital belongs to the owner.

## Lesson 5: Accounting for Investment Transactions (Fixed Assets Acquired)

**Note** (draft)

> Investment transactions buy fixed assets: items kept and used for more than one year (class 2): 231 Buildings, 241 Equipment, 244 Office furniture and equipment, 245 Transport equipment, 2183 Computer equipment.
> Purchase on credit: debit the asset account (HT), debit 4451 VAT recoverable on fixed assets, credit 481 Suppliers of fixed assets (TTC).

**Learn: Investing in fixed assets** (draft)

> A fixed asset is something the enterprise keeps and uses for more than one year to do its work: buildings, machines, office furniture, computers, vehicles. They are recorded in class 2, not as expenses.

## Lesson 6: Accounting for Trading Transactions (Purchases and Sales)

Prerequisites: 3 · Skill: a3-6 (Accounting for trading transactions: purchases and sales)

**Note** (draft)

> Trading transactions are the buying and selling of goods.
> • Purchase on credit: debit 601 Purchases of goods (HT), debit 445 VAT recoverable, credit 401 Suppliers (TTC).
> • Sale on credit: debit 411 Customers (TTC), credit 701 Sales of goods (HT), credit 443 VAT invoiced.
> • Cash sale or purchase: use 571 Cash (or 521 Bank) instead of 411 or 401.
> • Trade discount (rabais, remise): a reduction on the invoice; VAT is calculated on the net amount after discount.

**Learn: Buying goods** (draft)

> Goods bought for resale go to 601 Purchases of goods (amount excluding tax). The VAT paid goes to 445 VAT recoverable.
> If bought on credit, the supplier is owed the total: credit 401 Suppliers with the amount including tax. If paid at once, credit 571 Cash or 521 Bank.

**Learn: Selling goods** (draft)

> Sales of goods are income: credit 701 Sales of goods with the amount excluding tax and 443 VAT invoiced with the VAT.
> On credit, debit 411 Customers with the total including tax; for cash, debit 571 Cash.
> A sale of 200 000 FCFA HT: Dr 411 238 500, Cr 701 200 000, Cr 443 38 500.

**Learn: Trade discounts** (draft)

> A trade discount (rabais, remise) reduces the price on the invoice, for example for a regular customer or a large order.
> Work out the net amount first: list price − discount. Then calculate VAT on the net amount.
> 200 000 FCFA less 10%: net 180 000, VAT 34 650, total 214 650 FCFA.

**Learn: key words and examples** (draft)

- **purchases of goods**: account 601: goods bought to resell
- **sales of goods**: account 701: income from selling goods
- **trade discount**: a price reduction shown on the invoice
- **credit transaction**: paid later, not at once

## Lesson 7: Accounting for Transactions with Commercial Containers

Prerequisites: 6 · Skill: a3-7 (Accounting for transactions with commercial containers)

**Note** (draft)

> Commercial containers (emballages) such as crates, bottles and gas cylinders may be returnable. The customer pays a deposit (consignation) when goods are delivered in them and gets it back when they are returned.
> Seller:
> • Goods delivered with consigned containers: debit 411 Customers (goods TTC + deposit), credit 701 Sales and 443 VAT, and credit 4194 Customers, consigned containers (deposit). No VAT is charged on the deposit.
> • Containers returned: debit 4194, credit 411 (or 571 if refunded in cash) with the deposit refunded.
> Buyer: debit 4094 Suppliers, containers to be returned with the deposit paid; on return, credit 4094.
> Containers not returned are treated as sold (or bought).

**Learn: Returnable containers** (draft)

> Many goods come in returnable containers: crates of soft drinks and beer, gas cylinders, drums. The seller charges a deposit (consignation) to make sure the containers come back. When they are returned, the deposit is refunded.
> The deposit is not income and no VAT is charged on it: it is money the seller must give back.

**Learn: The seller's entries** (draft)

> • Containers delivered: debit 411 Customers, credit 4194 Customers, consigned containers with the deposits.
> • Containers returned: debit 4194, credit 411 Customers (or 571 Cash if refunded in cash).
> Example: 20 crates at 500 FCFA = 10 000 FCFA in deposits; 15 returned → refund 7500 FCFA.

**Learn: The buyer's entries and unreturned containers** (draft)

> The buyer records deposits paid in 4094 Suppliers, containers to be returned: debit when paid, credit when the containers go back.
> Containers that are not returned are treated as sold by the seller and bought by the buyer.
> Keeping track of deposits avoids losing money on missing crates.

**Learn: key words and examples** (draft)

- **commercial containers**: packaging such as crates, bottles and cylinders
- **deposit (consignation)**: money paid for returnable containers, refunded on return
- **4194**: seller's account for deposits on consigned containers
- **4094**: buyer's account for deposits paid on containers

## Lesson 8: Accounting for Cash Settlement Transactions

Prerequisites: 6 · Skill: a3-8 (Accounting for cash settlement transactions)

**Note** (draft)

> Settlement transactions pay or receive money owed:
> • Customer pays by cheque or transfer: debit 521 Bank, credit 411 Customers. In cash: debit 571 Cash.
> • Business pays a supplier: debit 401 Suppliers, credit 521 Bank (or 571 Cash).
> • Moving money between cash and bank: cash paid into the bank: debit 521 Bank, credit 571 Cash (often through 585 Transfers of funds).
> • Cash discount (escompte) for early payment: the seller records it as an expense (673 Discounts granted); the customer pays less.
> The cash and bank accounts must never show impossible balances; cash in hand cannot be negative.

**Learn: Settling debts** (draft)

> After a credit sale or purchase, someone still owes money. Settlement transactions pay these debts:
> • A customer pays: debit 521 Bank (cheque, transfer, mobile money to the business account) or 571 Cash; credit 411 Customers.
> • The business pays a supplier: debit 401 Suppliers; credit 521 Bank or 571 Cash.

**Learn: Moving money and keeping it safe** (draft)

> Cash paid into the bank: debit 521 Bank, credit 571 Cash. Cash withdrawn from the bank: the reverse. Many businesses use a transfer account (585) between the two.
> Keep track of the cash balance: opening cash + receipts − payments. It can never be negative: a credit balance on Cash shows an error.

**Learn: Cash discounts** (draft)

> To be paid sooner, a seller may offer a cash discount (escompte): for example 2% off if the customer pays within 10 days.
> The customer pays less; the seller records the discount as an expense (673 Discounts granted), and the customer as income.
> 100 000 FCFA with a 2% discount: the customer pays 98 000 FCFA.

**Learn: key words and examples** (draft)

- **settlement**: paying or receiving money owed
- **cash discount**: reduction for paying early
- **cash balance**: opening cash + receipts − payments

- Dr 401 / Cr 571 (supplier paid in cash)

## Lesson 10: Accounting for Transactions with Trade Bills

Prerequisites: 8 · Skill: a3-10 (Accounting for transactions with trade bills)

**Note** (draft)

> A trade bill (effet de commerce), such as a bill of exchange or a promissory note, is a written promise or order to pay a sum on a fixed date (maturity).
> Seller (drawer) who draws a bill on a customer:
> • Bill accepted: debit 412 Customers, bills receivable; credit 411 Customers.
> • Bill paid at maturity: debit 521 Bank; credit 412.
> Buyer who accepts a bill: debit 401 Suppliers; credit 402 Suppliers, bills payable. Paying it at maturity: debit 402; credit 521 Bank.
> Advantages: a clear date and legal proof of the debt; the seller can get cash earlier by discounting the bill at a bank.

**Learn: Paying later, in writing** (draft)

> When a customer cannot pay at once, the seller may ask for a trade bill (effet de commerce): a document in which the customer promises, or is ordered, to pay a sum on a fixed date, the maturity date.
> Bills of exchange and promissory notes are common trade bills. They give legal proof of the debt and a clear payment date.

**Learn: The seller's entries** (draft)

> • When the customer accepts the bill, the debt changes form: debit 412 Customers, bills receivable, credit 411 Customers.
> • At maturity, when the bill is paid: debit 521 Bank, credit 412.
> If the seller needs cash before maturity, it can discount the bill at a bank and receive the amount less interest.

**Learn: The buyer's entries** (draft)

> • Accepting a bill drawn by a supplier: debit 401 Suppliers, credit 402 Suppliers, bills payable.
> • Paying it at maturity: debit 402, credit 521 Bank.
> Each side moves the debt from the ordinary account (411 or 401) to the bills account (412 or 402).

**Learn: key words and examples** (draft)

- **trade bill**: a written promise or order to pay on a fixed date
- **maturity**: the date payment is due
- **bills receivable**: account 412: bills the business will collect
- **bills payable**: account 402: bills the business must pay

- Dr 412 / Cr 411 (seller: bill accepted)
- Dr 521 / Cr 412 (seller: bill paid)
- Dr 401 / Cr 402 (buyer: bill accepted)

Common mistake: “The seller records a new sale when the bill is accepted” → “Accepting a bill only changes the form of the existing debt: Dr 412 / Cr 411”. The sale was recorded earlier; the bill is just a new way of owing.
