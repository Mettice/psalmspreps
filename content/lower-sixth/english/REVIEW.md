## Lesson 3: Understanding Audience, Purpose, and Tone in Writing

Prerequisites: 2 · Skill: el6-3 (Audience, purpose and tone)

**Note** (draft)

> Audience is the intended reader or listener. Purpose is what the writer seeks to achieve, such as inform, request, persuade or entertain. Tone is the attitude conveyed through wording and structure. A text can have more than one purpose, but an interpretation needs evidence. Formal language does not automatically mean anger; a friendly tone does not prove the facts are reliable.

**Learn: Your goal and connections** (draft)

> By the end, you should be able to justify an audience-and-purpose reading using specific language choices. Builds on: main ideas and relevant details. Leads to: text types and directed writing.

**Learn: Understand the pattern** (draft)

> Audience is the intended reader or listener. Purpose is what the writer seeks to achieve, such as inform, request, persuade or entertain. Tone is the attitude conveyed through wording and structure. A text can have more than one purpose, but an interpretation needs evidence. Formal language does not automatically mean anger; a friendly tone does not prove the facts are reliable.

**Learn: Read the original model** (draft)

> Original notice to pupils: Please return borrowed equipment before the club closes so that the next group can prepare. Ask the organiser if you need more time. The request targets borrowers, gives a reason and offers a response. Its polite, practical tone differs from an insult or a claim that a penalty has already been imposed.

**Learn: Apply and check** (draft)

> Name the audience, identify the action or understanding sought and quote a short supporting phrase. Explain the tone through wording. Distinguish the author’s intention from proof that readers obeyed.

**Learn: key words and examples** (draft)

- **audience**: the people a text principally addresses
- **purpose**: the result the writer seeks through the text
- **tone**: the attitude conveyed by language choices
- **language evidence**: a particular phrase supporting an interpretation


Common mistake: “Every text is written for exactly the same readers.” → “audience: the people a text principally addresses.”. References and required actions identify likely readers.

## Lesson 13: Paraphrasing Techniques - Using One's Own Words

Prerequisites: 5, 12 · Skill: el6-13 (Paraphrasing without changing meaning)

## Lesson 23: Sentence Structure - Simple, Compound, and Complex Sentences

Prerequisites: 12, 22 · Skill: el6-23 (Simple, compound and complex sentences)

**Note** (draft)

> A simple sentence has one independent clause; it can still have a compound subject or verb. A compound sentence has at least two independent clauses. A complex sentence has an independent clause and at least one dependent clause. A compound-complex sentence combines at least two independent clauses with a dependent clause. Length and the mere presence of and do not determine the type. A comma alone does not normally join independent clauses in formal writing; use an appropriate conjunction or punctuation.

**Learn: Your goal and connections** (draft)

Prerequisites: 12, 24 · Skill: el6-25 (Comparatives and superlatives: form and scope)

**Note** (draft)

> Comparatives relate a quality across a comparison, as in clearer than this version. Superlatives identify an extreme within a stated group, as in the clearest of these drafts.
