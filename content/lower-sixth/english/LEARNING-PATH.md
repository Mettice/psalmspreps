# Lower Sixth English — learning path

Each opening gives a measurable end goal, earlier knowledge and next use. All passages are original. Sound work is written preparation, not listening or pronunciation assessment.

| Row | Topic | Outcome | Builds on | Leads to |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | Main ideas and supporting details | select a passage’s controlling idea and distinguish it from evidence or an unsupported claim | Form Five evidence-based reading | purpose, summary and inference |
| 3 | Audience, purpose and tone | justify an audience-and-purpose reading using specific language choices | main ideas and relevant details | text types and directed writing |
| 4 | Recognising narrative, descriptive, expository and argumentative texts | identify a dominant text function and recognise mixed features | audience and purpose | summary and purposeful composition |
| 5 | Selecting essential information for summary | retain central points while removing repetition, illustration and unsupported additions | main ideas and text functions | inference and paraphrase |
| 6 | Inference and conclusions from texts | justify a cautious inference and distinguish it from an unstated certainty | main ideas and summary fidelity | character interpretation and prose |
| 7 | Prose: character and setting | explain how an original prose passage presents character through action and setting | evidence-based inference | creative narrative and drama comparison |
| 8 | Written sound preparation: English monophthongs | match selected vowel symbols to model words and explain the limits of a written exercise | Form Five sound-symbol contrasts | oral practice and homophone distinctions |
| 9 | Formal and informal letters: purpose and register | write to a specified recipient with suitable register, a clear request and relevant support | audience, purpose and prior letter layouts | essay structure and directed writing |
| 10 | Nouns, pronouns and verbs in context | identify a word’s function in its sentence and maintain clear reference | Form Five parts of speech | modification, determiners and sentence control |
| 11 | Essay structure: a focused and developed line of thought | plan and draft an essay whose paragraphs develop its stated purpose | text types, summary selection and earlier essay structure | creative writing and later argument |
| 12 | Adjectives, adverbs, prepositions and conjunctions | identify modifiers and connectors from their function in context | nouns, pronouns and verbs | paraphrase and sentence relationships |
| 13 | Paraphrasing without changing meaning | restate an idea with changed wording and structure while preserving scope | summary fidelity and word functions | accurate directed writing and vocabulary |
| 14 | Determiners: articles, quantity and possession | choose a determiner that fits reference, countability and meaning | noun phrases and prior quantifiers | precise description and summary |
| 15 | Creative writing: purposeful invention | draft an original scene whose viewpoint, details and choices create a coherent effect | character, setting and essay organisation | drama and narrative reconstruction |
| 17 | Word formation: bases, prefixes and suffixes | analyse an affixed word in context and distinguish formation from a guessed letter sequence | word functions and paraphrase | lexical relations and precise word choice |
| 18 | Drama: plot, conflict and stage evidence | explain a dramatic conflict using dialogue, stage directions and changing action | prose characterisation and creative scenes | narrative reconstruction and later drama analysis |
| 19 | Synonyms, antonyms, homonyms and homophones | choose a lexical relationship in context and preserve meaning when substituting | word formation and model sound contrasts | narrative retelling and confusing words |
| 20 | Retelling texts in a coherent sequence | reconstruct events while preserving order, causes and the source’s limits | narrative features and lexical precision | word choice and narrative composition |
| 21 | Malapropism and words often confused | repair a contextually wrong near-sounding word and explain the intended meaning | lexical relationships and retelling | accurate narrative and editing |
| 22 | Narrative writing: choices, viewpoint and consequence | draft a coherent narrative in which choices produce an intelligible development | prose, creative scene and retelling | sentence variety and editing |
| 23 | Simple, compound and complex sentences | classify clause relationships and revise sentence structure without changing its meaning | conjunctions and coherent narrative | error diagnosis and descriptive control |
| 24 | Diagnosing grammatical errors in context | identify a specific error and make a meaning-preserving repair | word roles, clause structure and reference | comparison and descriptive drafting |
| 25 | Comparatives and superlatives: form and scope | form a comparison and distinguish a pairwise judgement from a group maximum | modifiers and editing | precise descriptive writing |
| 26 | Writing a focused descriptive passage | create an impression through selected sensory detail and coherent organisation | modification, comparison and narrative viewpoint | later extended description and stylistic analysis |

Row 1 diagnostic and row 16 explicit reading/grammar review are excluded under the authoring activity policy despite the parser calling them lessons. Twenty-four teaching drafts remain. Rows 9/11/17/22/23 explicitly extend Form Five foundations 32/7/29/20/11 with new goals, advanced comparison or scope commentary, and connections. Other rows are newly authored.

Writing rows 9/11/15/22/26 use paper tasks with original models and self-check criteria. Overall destination: read with evidence, preserve meaning when transforming text, and produce coherent original work for a specified purpose.
